# Exact rank-three gradient defect identity

This note isolates the remaining rank-three projection problem into one scalar
stability inequality.  Everything through the boxed identity below is exact;
the final stability inequality is the remaining theorem target.

Let `P` be a real `6 x 6` orthogonal projection of rank three.  Define

\[
y_i=P_{ii}-\frac12,
\qquad
x_{ij}=P_{ij}^2\quad(i\ne j),
\]

and

\[
S_2=\sum_i y_i^2,
\qquad
S_4=\sum_i y_i^4.
\]

Projection idempotence gives the exact row-square sums

\[
\sum_{j\ne i}x_{ij}
=P_{ii}-P_{ii}^2
=\frac14-y_i^2.
\tag{1}
\]

Define the two nonnegative energies

\[
W
=
\sum_i\sum_{\substack{j<k\\j,k\ne i}}x_{ij}x_{ik},
\tag{2}
\]

and

\[
L
=
\sum_{i<j}x_{ij}(y_i-y_j)^2
=\frac12\|[\operatorname{diag}(y),P]\|_F^2.
\tag{3}
\]

Here `W` measures how much squared off-diagonal mass spreads across multiple
edges at a vertex, while `L` measures failure of the diagonal imbalance to
commute with the projection.

For the six-variable hafnian gradient, write

\[
q_1(P)=\sum_{i<j}P_{ij}^2,
\qquad
q_2(P)=\sum_{i<j}
\operatorname{haf}(P_{\widehat i,\widehat j})^2.
\]

The desired rank-three contraction is

\[
q_2(P)\le\frac14q_1(P).
\tag{4}
\]

## Exact defect formula

### Theorem

Every rank-three projection satisfies

\[
\boxed{
\frac14q_1(P)-q_2(P)
=
W+\frac12L
-\frac18\left(S_2+S_2^2-10S_4\right).
}
\tag{5}
\]

Consequently (4) is exactly equivalent to

\[
\boxed{
8W+4L+10S_4\ge S_2+S_2^2.
}
\tag{6}
\]

Thus the entire unresolved rank-three projection theorem is now one explicit
stability inequality between two manifestly nonnegative geometric energies and
the diagonal imbalance moments.

## Proof of the identity

Set

\[
K=2P-I.
\]

Then `K` is symmetric orthogonal with `K^2=I` and `Tr(K)=0`.  Write

\[
z_i=K_{ii}=2y_i,
\qquad
w_{ij}=K_{ij}^2=4x_{ij}.
\]

For an edge `{i,j}` define

\[
s_{ij}
=
\sum_{k\ne i,j}K_{ik}K_{jk}.
\]

The off-diagonal entries of `K^2=I` give

\[
s_{ij}=-(z_i+z_j)K_{ij}.
\tag{7}
\]

Let `D` be the sum of `w_e w_f` over unordered disjoint edge pairs and let
`C_4` be the sum of the corresponding signed four-cycle products.  Expanding
every squared complementary `4 x 4` hafnian gives

\[
q_2(K)=D+2C_4.
\tag{8}
\]

On the other hand, squaring (7) and summing over all edges counts every
adjacent squared-edge pair once and every signed four-cycle twice through its
two opposite vertex pairs.  If

\[
W_K
=
\sum_i\sum_{j<k}w_{ij}w_{ik},
\]

then

\[
\sum_{i<j}s_{ij}^2=W_K+4C_4.
\tag{9}
\]

Also, partitioning all unordered pairs of distinct edges into adjacent and
disjoint pairs yields

\[
D
=
\frac12\left(q_1(K)^2-\sum_{i<j}w_{ij}^2\right)-W_K.
\tag{10}
\]

Equations (8)--(10), together with

\[
\sum_{j\ne i}w_{ij}=1-z_i^2,
\]

reduce `q2(K)` to diagonal moments, squared edge weights and

\[
L_K=\sum_{i<j}w_{ij}(z_i-z_j)^2.
\]

After collecting terms one obtains

\[
q_2(K)
=
\sum_{i<j}w_{ij}^2
+Z_2+\frac18Z_2^2
-\frac74Z_4
-\frac12L_K,
\tag{11}
\]

where `Z_2=sum z_i^2` and `Z_4=sum z_i^4`.

Since

\[
q_1(K)=3-\frac12Z_2,
\]

and

\[
W_K
=
\frac12\sum_i(1-z_i^2)^2
-\sum_{i<j}w_{ij}^2,
\]

substitution into (11), followed by the scalings

\[
q_1(P)=\frac14q_1(K),
\quad
q_2(P)=\frac1{16}q_2(K),
\quad
Z_2=4S_2,
\quad
Z_4=16S_4,
\quad
W_K=16W,
\quad
L_K=16L,
\]

gives exactly (5).

## Equal-diagonal case

When `P_ii=1/2` for every `i`, we have `S2=S4=L=0`, so (5) reduces to

\[
\frac14q_1(P)-q_2(P)=W\ge0.
\]

The previously proved equal-diagonal rank-three theorem is therefore the
zero-imbalance boundary of the general defect identity.  Equality forces
`W=0`, which gives the signed perfect-matching / disjoint-pair geometry.

## A false stronger route

The perfect-matching operator `T(A)` from `projection_gradient.md` satisfies
`T(A)a=2h` on the specific edge vector `a` generated by `A`.  It was natural
to ask whether the stronger operator bound

\[
\|T(K)\|_{op}\le1
\]

might hold for every symmetric orthogonal rank-three involution `K`.
It does not.

For the exact conference involution

\[
K=S/\sqrt5,
\qquad
S^2=5I,
\]

used in the six-variable witness calculations, direct diagonalization of the
15-edge matching operator gives

\[
\boxed{
\|T(K)\|_{op}=\frac3{\sqrt5}>1.
}
\]

The repository contains an exact CPU test for this counterexample.  Therefore
the proof must exploit the fact that the contraction is needed only on the
specific edge vector of `K`, not on the entire 15-dimensional edge space.

## Current target

The next proof task is now precisely (6):

\[
8W+4L+10S_4\ge S_2+S_2^2.
\]

Two weaker relaxations have already been ruled out computationally:

1. the row-sum constraints (1) alone are insufficient;
2. a generic operator-norm contraction for `T(K)` is false.

The useful remaining structure is the full projection geometry, including the
sharp two-coordinate capacities

\[
P_{ij}^2
\le
\min\{P_{ii}P_{jj},(1-P_{ii})(1-P_{jj})\},
\]

plus the signed off-diagonal identities from `P^2=P` / `K^2=I`.
