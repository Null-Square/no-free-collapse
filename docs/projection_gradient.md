# Six-variable hafnian gradient structure

This note records a finite-dimensional reformulation of the remaining
six-variable hafnian problem.  It separates what is proved from a stronger
operator-norm statement that is only a candidate proof route.

Let `A` be a real symmetric `6 x 6` matrix.  For an edge `e={i,j}` write

\[
a_e=A_{ij},
\]

and define the complementary four-variable hafnian

\[
h_e=\operatorname{haf}(A_{\widehat i,\widehat j}).
\]

Set

\[
q_1(A)=\sum_e a_e^2,
\qquad
q_2(A)=\sum_e h_e^2.
\]

The candidate gradient contraction used in the six-variable proof program is

\[
q_2(A)\le \frac{\lambda_{\max}(A)^2}{4}q_1(A)
\tag{G}
\]

for `A>=0`.  Equation (G) is **not proved globally** here.

## Weighted perfect-matching operator

Index rows and columns by the 15 edges of `K_6`.  Define `T(A)` by

\[
T(A)_{e,f}=0
\]

when `e` and `f` intersect.  When they are disjoint, there is a unique third
edge `g` such that `{e,f,g}` is a perfect matching of the six vertices, and we
set

\[
T(A)_{e,f}=a_g.
\]

This matrix is symmetric.  If `a` and `h` denote the 15-vectors above, then

\[
\boxed{T(A)a=2h.}
\tag{1}
\]

Indeed, for fixed `e`, the six edges disjoint from `e` come in three
complementary pairs.  Each perfect matching of the four remaining vertices is
therefore counted twice.

Consequently

\[
4q_2(A)=\|T(A)a\|_2^2,
\qquad
q_1(A)=\|a\|_2^2.
\tag{2}
\]

Thus (G) is exactly the contraction inequality

\[
\|T(A)a\|_2\le \lambda_{\max}(A)\|a\|_2
\tag{3}
\]

for the **specific edge vector generated by `A`**.

The stronger statement

\[
\|T(A)\|_{op}\le\lambda_{\max}(A)
\tag{4}
\]

would imply (G), but (4) is only a candidate route and is not asserted as a
theorem.

## Square-free / zeon interpretation

Let `z_1,...,z_6` commute and satisfy `z_i^2=0`, and put

\[
\Omega_A=\sum_{i<j}A_{ij}z_i z_j.
\]

In the Euclidean coefficient norm,

\[
\|\Omega_A\|^2=q_1(A).
\]

The coefficient of `z_i z_j z_k z_l` in `Omega_A^2` is twice the hafnian of
the corresponding `4 x 4` principal submatrix, hence

\[
\|\Omega_A^2\|^2=4q_2(A).
\]

So (G) can equivalently be written

\[
\|\Omega_A^2\|\le\lambda_{\max}(A)\|\Omega_A\|.
\]

The terminology and general norm theory of zeon algebras are established
prior art; the point here is only the exact identification of our
six-variable gradient quantity with this multiplication problem.

## Exact theorem for rank-one projections

### Theorem

Let `P=uu^T` be a rank-one orthogonal projection in `R^6`, so
`sum_i u_i^2=1`.  Then

\[
\boxed{q_2(P)\le\frac14 q_1(P).}
\tag{5}
\]

For a nonzero rank-one projection, equality holds exactly when

\[
u_1^2=\cdots=u_6^2=\frac16.
\]

### Proof

Write

\[
d_i=u_i^2,
\qquad \sum_i d_i=1.
\]

Then

\[
P_{ij}^2=d_i d_j,
\]

so

\[
q_1(P)=e_2(d).
\tag{6}
\]

For a complementary four-set `{a,b,c,d}`, each of the three matching terms in
its hafnian is the same product `u_a u_b u_c u_d`.  Therefore

\[
h_{ij}=3\prod_{k\ne i,j}u_k
\]

and hence

\[
q_2(P)=9e_4(d).
\tag{7}
\]

Maclaurin's inequalities give

\[
\frac{e_4(d)}{\binom64}
\le
\left(\frac{e_2(d)}{\binom62}\right)^2,
\]

that is,

\[
e_4(d)\le\frac{e_2(d)^2}{15}.
\]

Also

\[
e_2(d)=\frac{1-\sum_i d_i^2}{2}\le\frac5{12}
\]

by Cauchy-Schwarz.  Combining the two estimates,

\[
q_2(P)=9e_4(d)
\le\frac35 e_2(d)^2
\le\frac14 e_2(d)
=\frac14 q_1(P).
\]

Equality in both Maclaurin and Cauchy requires all six `d_i` to be equal.

## Rank-five theorem by complementation

If `P` is any orthogonal projection, let `P^c=I-P`.  Off-diagonal entries
change sign:

\[
P^c_{ij}=-P_{ij},\qquad i\ne j.
\]

Therefore

\[
q_1(P^c)=q_1(P).
\]

Each complementary four-variable hafnian is quadratic in off-diagonal
entries, so the two sign changes in every matching term cancel and

\[
q_2(P^c)=q_2(P).
\]

The rank-one theorem therefore gives the same sharp `1/4` contraction for
rank-five projections.  Ranks zero and six are trivial.  Consequently the
projection problem reduces to ranks two and three (rank four is the complement
of rank two).

## Exact rank-three involution reduction

Let `P` have rank three and define

\[
K=2P-I.
\]

Then

\[
K^T=K,
\qquad
K^2=I,
\qquad
\operatorname{tr}K=0.
\]

Conversely every real symmetric orthogonal `6 x 6` matrix with trace zero
arises this way.

For `i != j`,

\[
P_{ij}=\frac12K_{ij}.
\]

Therefore

\[
q_1(P)=\frac14q_1(K),
\qquad
q_2(P)=\frac1{16}q_2(K).
\]

Hence the rank-three case of (G), where `lambda_max(P)=1`, is exactly

\[
\boxed{q_2(K)\le q_1(K)}
\tag{8}
\]

for symmetric orthogonal `K` with trace zero.

This removes the projector equations `P^2=P` in favor of the cleaner
involution identities `K^2=I` and `tr K=0`.

## Weighted-graph / commutator coordinates for rank three

For a rank-three projection write

\[
d_i=P_{ii},\qquad y_i=d_i-\frac12,\qquad x_{ij}=P_{ij}^2.
\]

Then

\[
\sum_i y_i=0,
\qquad
\sum_{j\ne i}x_{ij}=d_i(1-d_i)=\frac14-y_i^2.
\]

If `L_x` is the weighted graph Laplacian with edge weights `x_ij`, then

\[
y^TL_xy
=\sum_{i<j}x_{ij}(y_i-y_j)^2
=\frac12\|[\operatorname{diag}(y),P]\|_F^2.
\]

This gives a nonnegative measure of how diagonal imbalance fails to commute
with the projection.  Together with the nonnegative wedge-spreading term

\[
\frac12\sum_i\left(\sum_{j\ne i}x_{ij}\right)^2
-\sum_{i<j}x_{ij}^2
=\sum_i\sum_{j<k}x_{ij}x_{ik},
\]

it provides a concrete route to the remaining rank-three inequality.  No
claim that these terms already prove (8) is made here.

## Prior-art boundary

Hafnians, generalized Laplace expansions, averages of squared subhafnians, and
zeon norm inequalities are established topics.  In particular, B. Roos,
*New inequalities for permanents and hafnians and some generalizations*,
Linear and Multilinear Algebra 73 (2025), develops upper bounds involving
squared four-variable subhafnians.  Lindell and Staples,
*Norm Inequalities in Zeon Algebras*, Advances in Applied Clifford Algebras 29
(2019), develops general zeon norm inequalities.

The present note does not claim those tools as new.  Its role is to isolate the
specific sharp contraction needed by the six-variable PSD capacity problem and
to record the exact rank reductions that have been proved.
