\begin{abstract}
A matrix-weighted Kneser operator on $(m-1)$-subsets of $[2m]$ is shown to
satisfy a sharp spectral-diameter bound, proved by an explicit
complementary-subset dilation. For a real symmetric matrix $A$ of order $N$,
write $d(A)=\lambda_{\max}(A)-\lambda_{\min}(A)$ and let $\overline H_{2r}(A)$
be the average of $|\haf(A[S])|^2$ over its $2r$-element principal subsets.
The dilation yields
$\overline H_{2r}(A)\le d(A)^2\overline H_{2r-2}(A)/4$
for every integer $r\ge2$ with $N\ge4r-2$. The one-step constant is sharp
at each order, with equality attained at the critical dimension by balanced
rank-one projections, their complements, and matching projections.
Combining the spectral recurrence with Roos's subhafnian inequality gives a
one-switch product bound. A sharp fixed-dimension edge-density estimate,
including odd dimensions, identifies when its spectral factor is smaller.
On critical matching projections the improvement in the one-step coefficient
grows linearly with the hafnian order. In dimension six, all PSD equality
cases of the associated gradient and energy bounds are classified.
A corollary controls averaged unconditional collision-free probabilities for
real Gaussian boson sampling. Exact rational certificates and separate
floating-point diagnostics support reproducibility; they are not used in
place of the analytic proofs.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} hafnian; subhafnian; Kneser operator; spectral diameter;
positive semidefinite matrix; dilation.

\section{Introduction}
For a symmetric matrix of even order, the hafnian is the sum of products of
entries over perfect matchings.  Squared hafnians of principal submatrices
therefore measure, in an $\ell^2$ sense, the aggregate weight of matching
amplitudes across induced vertex sets.  Such quantities appear naturally in
hafnian inequalities and, for collision-free Gaussian boson sampling (GBS),
in output probabilities~\cite{hamilton,kruse,arrazola}.

Recent work of Roos~\cite{roos} develops a log-subadditive hierarchy for
normalized averages of squared subhafnians using generalized Laplace
expansions.  The bounds depend on lower-order entry energies, but not on the
spectrum of the underlying matrix.  Our starting point is a different
question: how much can a matrix's spectral diameter constrain a weighted
matching operator on the Boolean lattice?  An explicit signed dilation answers
that question sharply and, unexpectedly, produces a second hierarchy for
subhafnian energies.  The two hierarchies are complementary and can be combined.

For $A=A^T\in\R^{N\times N}$ define
\[
d(A)=\lambda_{\max}(A)-\lambda_{\min}(A),\qquad
H_{2r}(A)=\sum_{\substack{S\subseteq[N]\\|S|=2r}}|\haf(A[S])|^2,
\qquad
\Hbar_{2r}(A)=\frac{H_{2r}(A)}{\binom N{2r}}.
\]
Our main consequences are the following.
\begin{itemize}
\item A sharp all-even-dimensional spectral theorem for a matrix-weighted
Kneser operator, with explicit positive-semidefinite slack factorizations.
\item The spectral hierarchy
$\Hbar_{2r}\le d(A)^2\Hbar_{2r-2}/4$ for $N\ge4r-2$, sharp for every $r$.
\item A hybrid with Roos's log-subadditivity that has a single density-to-spectrum
crossover and cannot be further improved merely by reapplying larger Roos
partitions to the same upper bounds.
\item A sharp edge-density bound identifying the regime in which the spectral
factor can improve the Roos factor.
\item In dimension six, the full PSD hafnian-gradient inequality together with
all equality cases.
\item A collision-free GBS corollary controlling average unconditional
per-pattern probability across low photon sectors.
\end{itemize}

Kneser adjacency and Boolean-lattice operators are classical.  Kummer~\cite{kummer}
uses an unweighted Kneser adjacency operator in a square-free derivative
isomorphism, and Feinsilver~\cite{feinsilver} studies $\mathfrak{sl}(2)$
representations on the Boolean lattice.  Zeon norm inequalities are developed
by Lindell and Staples~\cite{lindell}.  The theorem here concerns a specified
matrix-weighted operator and a sharp bound by the spectral diameter.  Section
\ref{sec:comparison} gives a proposition-level comparison with Roos and records
limits of the priority claim.

\section{A complementary-subset dilation}
Let $n=2m$, $m\ge2$, and write $\mathcal E=\binom{[n]}{m-1}$.
For real symmetric $A\in\R^{n\times n}$ define the symmetric matrix
$\mathcal T_m(A)$ on $\R^{\mathcal E}$ by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:operator}
\mathcal T_m(A)_{E,F}=
\begin{cases}
a_{ij},&E\cap F=\varnothing,\quad[n]\setminus(E\cup F)=\{i,j\},\\
0,&E\cap F\ne\varnothing.
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
Thus diagonal entries of $A$ do not enter $\mathcal T_m(A)$.
For each $m$-subset $R$, let $c_R:\R^{\mathcal E}\to\R^n$ be
\[
(c_R(v))_i=\begin{cases}v_{R\setminus\{i\}},&i\in R,\\0,&i\notin R.\end{cases}
\]
Choose one representative from each complementary pair $\{R,R^c\}$ and
stack the maps $c_R+c_{R^c}$ and $c_R-c_{R^c}$ to form $L_+$ and $L_-$.

\begin{lemma}[Exact complementary-subset dilation]\label{lem:dilation}
The incidence maps satisfy
\[
L_+^TL_+=L_-^TL_-=(m+1)I,
\]
and, with $M=\binom{2m}{m}/2$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dilation}
\mathcal T_m(A)=\frac14\left[
L_+^T(I_M\otimes A)L_+-L_-^T(I_M\otimes A)L_-
\right].
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Each row of either incidence map contains exactly one coefficient of modulus
one.  A fixed $(m-1)$-subset is contained in precisely $m+1$ different
$m$-subsets, proving the Gram identities.  For one complementary pair,
\[
(c_R+c_{R^c})^TA(c_R+c_{R^c})-
(c_R-c_{R^c})^TA(c_R-c_{R^c})=4c_R^TAc_{R^c}.
\]
For fixed disjoint $(m-1)$-subsets $E,F$, the two remaining vertices can be
assigned to the two complementary $m$-sets in two orders; these are exactly
the two occurrences in the quadratic form $v^T\mathcal T_m(A)v$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Sharp weighted Kneser spectral bound]\label{thm:operator}
For every real symmetric $A$ of order $2m$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:operatorbound}
\boxed{
\|\mathcal T_m(A)\|_{\op}\le\frac{m+1}{4}d(A).
}
\end{equation}
The coefficient $(m+1)/4$ is optimal for every $m\ge2$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
First assume $0\preceq A\preceq\ell I$ and put $c=(m+1)/4$.
Lemma~\ref{lem:dilation} gives the explicit positive-semidefinite identities
\begin{align}
c\ell I-\mathcal T_m(A)
&=\tfrac14\left[L_+^T(I_M\otimes(\ell I-A))L_+
+L_-^T(I_M\otimes A)L_-\right],\label{eq:minus}\\
c\ell I+\mathcal T_m(A)
&=\tfrac14\left[L_-^T(I_M\otimes(\ell I-A))L_-
+L_+^T(I_M\otimes A)L_+\right].\label{eq:plus}
\end{align}
Hence $\|\mathcal T_m(A)\|\le c\ell$.  For general $A$, subtract
$\lambda_{\min}(A)I$, which leaves $\mathcal T_m$ unchanged, and set
$\ell=d(A)$.  Sharpness follows from $A=J_{2m}/(2m)$: every row of
$\mathcal T_m(A)$ has $\binom{m+1}{2}$ entries equal to $1/(2m)$, so its
all-ones eigenvector has eigenvalue $(m+1)/4$, while $d(A)=1$.
\end{proof}

\section{A spectral hierarchy for principal subhafnian energies}
\subsection{The critical dimension}
The operator theorem becomes a hafnian recursion at a distinguished layer of
the Boolean lattice.

\begin{theorem}[Critical subhafnian step]\label{thm:critical}
Let $r\ge2$ and let $B=B^T$ be real of order $4r-2$.  Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:critical}
\boxed{H_{2r}(B)\le\frac{d(B)^2}{4}H_{2r-2}(B).}
\end{equation}
The constant $1/4$ is sharp for every $r$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Set $m=2r-1$ and index a vector $v$ by the $(2r-2)$-subsets $E$ by
$v_E=\haf(B[E])$.  For fixed $E$, its complement has $2r$ vertices, and
summing the hafnian Laplace expansion over all possible first edges counts
each perfect matching once for each of its $r$ edges.  Hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hafrec}
(\mathcal T_m(B)v)_E
=\sum_{\{i,j\}\subseteq E^c}b_{ij}
\haf(B[E^c\setminus\{i,j\}])
=r\,\haf(B[E^c]).
\end{equation}
Complementation is a bijection between the $(2r-2)$- and $2r$-subsets of
$[4r-2]$, so
\[
\|v\|^2=H_{2r-2}(B),\qquad
\|\mathcal T_m(B)v\|^2=r^2H_{2r}(B).
\]
Theorem~\ref{thm:operator} gives
$\|\mathcal T_m(B)\|\le r d(B)/2$.  Squaring and cancelling $r^2$ proves
\eqref{eq:critical}.

For sharpness put $N=4r-2$.  Three PSD contractions attain equality:
$ss^T/N$, $I-ss^T/N$, and $(I+M)/2$, where $s\in\{\pm1\}^N$ and $M$ is a
signed perfect-matching matrix.  For the first family,
\[
H_{2q}=\binom N{2q}\frac{((2q-1)!!)^2}{N^{2q}},
\]
and for the matching family,
\[
H_{2q}=\binom{N/2}{q}4^{-q}.
\]
At $N=4r-2$, the relevant consecutive binomial coefficients coincide and the
ratio in \eqref{eq:critical} is exactly $1/4$.  The complement has the same
even-order squared hafnians as the rank-one family.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Arbitrary ambient dimension}
\begin{theorem}[Averaged spectral hierarchy]\label{thm:hierarchy}
Let $A=A^T\in\R^{N\times N}$.  If $r\ge2$ and $N\ge4r-2$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hierarchy}
\boxed{
\Hbar_{2r}(A)\le\frac{d(A)^2}{4}\Hbar_{2r-2}(A).
}
\end{equation}
Consequently,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:iterate}
\boxed{
\Hbar_{2r}(A)\le
\left(\frac{d(A)^2}{4}\right)^{r-1}\Hbar_2(A).
}
\end{equation}
The one-step constant $1/4$ is optimal uniformly over the admissible pairs
$(N,r)$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:critical} to every principal subset $U$ of size
$4r-2$.  Cauchy interlacing gives $d(A[U])\le d(A)$.  Each $2r$-subset is
contained in $\binom{N-2r}{2r-2}$ such $U$, while each $(2r-2)$-subset is
contained in $\binom{N-2r+2}{2r}$ such $U$.  Therefore
\[
\binom{N-2r}{2r-2}H_{2r}(A)
\le\frac{d(A)^2}{4}
\binom{N-2r+2}{2r}H_{2r-2}(A).
\]
The ratio of the two multiplicities is
$\binom N{2r}/\binom N{2r-2}$, giving \eqref{eq:hierarchy}.  Iteration proves
\eqref{eq:iterate}.  Uniform sharpness follows from the critical examples in
Theorem~\ref{thm:critical}.  We do not claim that $1/4$ is the best constant
for each fixed $N>4r-2$.
\end{proof}

One may equivalently view \eqref{eq:hierarchy} as a monotonicity law: for
fixed $A$ with $d(A)>0$, the quantities
\[
\frac{4^r\Hbar_{2r}(A)}{d(A)^{2r}}
\]
are nonincreasing for $1\le r\le\lfloor(N+2)/4\rfloor$.
When $d(A)=0$, the matrix is scalar and every positive-order principal hafnian vanishes.

\section{Hybridization with Roos's subhafnian inequality}\label{sec:hybrid}
Following Roos~\cite{roos}, define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:G}
G_r(A)=\frac{\Hbar_{2r}(A)}{((2r-1)!!)^2}.
\end{equation}
Then Theorem~\ref{thm:hierarchy} becomes
\begin{equation}\label{eq:spectralG}
\boxed{
G_r(A)\le\frac{d(A)^2}{4(2r-1)^2}G_{r-1}(A).
}
\end{equation}
Roos's Theorem~2.3 is logarithmically subadditive and in particular gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:roos}
G_r(A)\le G_{r-1}(A)G_1(A).
\end{equation}
The two statements use different information. Roos states Theorem~2.3
for even ambient order. The following explicit use of his Corollary~3.1
justifies the same recurrence for odd ambient order as well.

\begin{lemma}[Parity-free use of Roos's convolution inequality]\label{lem:roosparity}
For any complex symmetric matrix of order $N$ and positive integers $a,b$
with $2(a+b)\le N$, one has $G_{a+b}\le G_aG_b$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let $F_j(S)=\haf(A[S])/(2j-1)!!$ on sets of size $2j$ and put $r=a+b$.
Partitioning a perfect matching into $a$ edges and $b$ edges gives
\[
\sum_{\substack{T\subset S\\|T|=2a}}
\haf(A[T])\haf(A[S\setminus T])=\binom ra\haf(A[S]).
\]
Since
$\binom ra(2r-1)!!=\binom{2r}{2a}(2a-1)!!(2b-1)!!$, this is equivalent to
\[
F_r(S)=\binom{2r}{2a}^{-1}
\sum_{\substack{T\subset S\\|T|=2a}}F_a(T)F_b(S\setminus T).
\]
Roos's Corollary~3.1 applies with ambient set $[N]$, subset size $2r$,
and block sizes $(2a,2b)$. It bounds the average squared normalized
convolution by the product of the two average squared factors. No parity
condition on $N$ is needed in that corollary.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[One-switch hybrid]\label{cor:hybrid}
If $r\ge2$ and $N\ge4r-2$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hybrid}
\boxed{
G_r(A)\le G_1(A)\prod_{s=2}^r
\min\left\{G_1(A),\frac{d(A)^2}{4(2s-1)^2}\right\}.
}
\end{equation}
The selected factor switches from the Roos term to the spectral term at most
once.  If $k$ is the last order at which the Roos factor is selected, with
ties assigned to Roos, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:closedhybrid}
\boxed{
G_r(A)\le G_1(A)^k
\left(\frac{d(A)^2}{4}\right)^{r-k}
\left(\frac{(2k-1)!!}{(2r-1)!!}\right)^2.
}
\end{equation}
Moreover, the upper bounds on the right of \eqref{eq:hybrid} are themselves
log-subadditive, so reapplying Roos's theorem with larger partitions cannot
improve this chain using only these same lower-order bounds.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Equation~\eqref{eq:hybrid} follows by choosing the smaller factor in
\eqref{eq:spectralG} and \eqref{eq:roos} at every step.  The spectral factors
$d(A)^2/[4(2s-1)^2]$ decrease with $s$, whereas $G_1(A)$ is fixed, so there is
at most one switch, and the product of the spectral tail gives
\eqref{eq:closedhybrid}.

If $G_1=0$, every off-diagonal entry is zero and the conclusion is trivial.
Otherwise, for the last claim, write $U_s$ for the right side of \eqref{eq:hybrid} and
$m_j=\min\{G_1,d(A)^2/[4(2j-1)^2]\}$.  Suppose $a\ge b$.  Then
\[
\frac{U_{a+b}}{U_aU_b}
=\frac{\prod_{j=a+1}^{a+b}m_j}
{G_1\prod_{j=2}^{b}m_j}\le1,
\]
because $m_{a+1}\le G_1$ and $m_{a+j}\le m_j$ for $2\le j\le b$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{A sharp density crossover}
For $d(A)>0$, define
\[
\theta(A)=\frac{4G_1(A)}{d(A)^2}.
\]
The spectral step \eqref{eq:spectralG} beats the Roos step
\eqref{eq:roos} exactly when
\begin{equation}\label{eq:crossover}
\theta(A)>\frac1{(2r-1)^2}.
\end{equation}
The parameter $\theta$ has a sharp universal ceiling.

\begin{proposition}[Sharp fixed-order spectral edge density]\label{prop:theta}
For every non-scalar real symmetric matrix $A$ of order $N\ge2$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:theta}
\boxed{\theta(A)\le\frac{4\lfloor N^2/4\rfloor}{N^2(N-1)}
=\begin{cases}1/(N-1),&N\text{ even},\\(N+1)/N^2,&N\text{ odd}.\end{cases}}
\end{equation}
Equality holds exactly when
$B=(A-\lambda_{\min}(A)I)/d(A)$ is a constant-diagonal orthogonal
projection of rank $\lfloor N/2\rfloor$ or $\lceil N/2\rceil$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Write the eigenvalues of $B$ as $\lambda_i\in[0,1]$ and let $t=\tr B$.
Cauchy--Schwarz on the diagonal gives
\[
2H_2(B)=\tr(B^2)-\sum_iB_{ii}^2
\le\sum_i\lambda_i^2-\frac{(\sum_i\lambda_i)^2}{N}.
\]
The last expression is strictly convex in each eigenvalue separately, so
its maximum on $[0,1]^N$ is attained only at vertices. At a vertex with $k$
ones it is $k(N-k)/N$, maximized at $k=\lfloor N/2\rfloor$ or
$\lceil N/2\rceil$. Divide by $\binom N2$ and use
$\theta(A)=4G_1(B)$ to obtain \eqref{eq:theta}.
Equality requires every eigenvalue to be an endpoint and every diagonal
entry to be $k/N$, and these conditions suffice. Such real projections
exist: for even $N$ use the matching projection; for odd $N$ use $k$ columns
of the real Fourier basis, choosing sine/cosine pairs and including the
constant column exactly when $k$ is odd. Each selected pair contributes
$2/N$ to every diagonal entry and the constant column contributes $1/N$.
\end{proof}

Equations~\eqref{eq:crossover}--\eqref{eq:theta} show that the new spectral
step can improve Roos only if
$4\lfloor N^2/4\rfloor/[N^2(N-1)]>1/(2r-1)^2$.
A simpler necessary window, exact for this ceiling when $N$ is even, is
\[
4r-2\le N<(2r-1)^2+1.
\]
At the critical order $N=4r-2$, the balanced rank-one projection has
$\theta=1/(2r-1)^2$ and therefore sits exactly at the crossover, attaining both
one-step bounds.  A matching projection has the maximal
$\theta=1/(4r-3)$, and the Roos coefficient is then larger than the spectral
coefficient by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:factor}
\boxed{\frac{(2r-1)^2}{4r-3}=\Theta(r).}
\end{equation}
Thus neither result is a reparameterization of the other: the dense constant
off-diagonal geometry is sharp for Roos, while a sparse half-rank matching
geometry is sharp for the spectral step and yields an asymptotically growing
one-step improvement.

\section{The six-variable PSD theorem and all equality cases}
For a real symmetric $6\times6$ matrix define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:q}
q_1(A)=\sum_{i<j}a_{ij}^2,\qquad
q_2(A)=\sum_{i<j}\haf(A_{\widehat i,\widehat j})^2.
\end{equation}
When $m=3$, the coordinates of $\mathcal T_3(A)$ are the fifteen edges of
$K_6$.  If $a=(a_{ij})_{i<j}$ and
$h=(\haf(A_{\widehat i,\widehat j}))_{i<j}$, then direct matching enumeration
gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gradient}
\mathcal T_3(A)a=2h,\qquad a^Th=3\haf(A).
\end{equation}
Therefore Theorem~\ref{thm:operator} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:six}
\boxed{
q_2(A)\le\frac{d(A)^2}{4}q_1(A),\qquad
|\haf(A)|\le\frac{d(A)}6q_1(A).
}
\end{equation}
For PSD $A$, $d(A)\le\lambda_{\max}(A)$, so this closes the PSD-contraction
inequality that motivated the operator problem.

A signed perfect-matching matrix is a real symmetric zero-diagonal matrix
with exactly one entry $\pm1$ in each row, arranged in three disjoint edges.

\begin{theorem}[PSD equality classification in order six]\label{thm:equality}
Let $A\ne0$ be PSD of order six and let $\ell=\lambda_{\max}(A)$.  Equality in
$\|\mathcal T_3(A)\|\le\ell$ holds exactly when
\begin{equation}\label{eq:families}
\boxed{
A/\ell=ss^T/6,\qquad I-ss^T/6,\qquad (I+M)/2,
}
\end{equation}
where $s\in\{\pm1\}^6$ and $M$ is a signed perfect-matching matrix.  For either
scalar inequality in \eqref{eq:six} with the PSD bound $d(A)\le\ell$, these
are all nondegenerate equality cases; diagonal PSD matrices additionally give
the degenerate equality $q_1=q_2=\haf(A)=0$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Normalize $\ell=1$ and suppose a unit real edge vector $v$ attains
$v^T\mathcal T_3(A)v=\pm1$.  Let $C$ be the $6\times20$ matrix whose columns
are $c_R(v)$ over all triples, let $J$ exchange complementary triples, and put
$P_\pm=(I\pm J)/2$.  Define
\[
H=\tfrac12CJC^T=H_+-H_-,\qquad H_\pm=\tfrac12CP_\pm C^T.
\]
The matrix $H$ has zero diagonal and complementary four-variable hafnians as
off-diagonal entries.  Since $\|C\|_F^2=4$,
$\tr H_+=\tr H_-=1$, and $v^T\mathcal T_3(A)v=\tr(AH)$.
Equality in the two PSD slack terms forces $A$ to act as one on the range of
$CP_+$ and zero on the range of $CP_-$, or conversely.  These ranges are
orthogonal, so $C^TC$ commutes with $J$.

The diagonal part of this commutation gives, for every triple $R$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:balance}
\sum_{\{i,j\}\subset R}v_{ij}^2
=\sum_{\{i,j\}\subset R^c}v_{ij}^2.
\end{equation}
Comparing entries corresponding to triples $\{i,j,k\}$ and $\{i,j,l\}$ and
varying the remaining pair gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:products}
v_{ak}v_{al}\quad\text{independent of }a\notin\{k,l\}.
\end{equation}
Here are the details of the support and sign argument. If a vertex has
neighbors $k,l$, the nonzero product in \eqref{eq:products} forces both
$k,l$ adjacent to every other vertex. Applying the same condition to two
of the other four vertices makes those four a clique; an application to
$k$ and one of those four then forces the edge $k,l$. The support is complete.
Otherwise the support is a matching. Fix a nonzero edge $ab$. For each of
the four remaining vertices $c$, the balance identity for $\{a,b,c\}$
requires a nonzero edge of equal squared weight among the other three
vertices. One remaining edge cannot meet all four requirements, so there
are exactly two remaining disjoint edges, each of the same modulus as $ab$.

In the complete case, fix $a,b$ and let $r_k=v_{ak}/v_{bk}$ for the four
indices outside $\{a,b\}$. Equation~\eqref{eq:products} gives $r_kr_l=1$
for all distinct $k,l$, so all these ratios coincide and belong to $\{1,-1\}$.
All incident moduli, and therefore all edge moduli, coincide. To verify
sign consistency explicitly, fix vertex $1$ and write
$v_{ak}=t_a v_{1k}$ for $a,k\ne1$, $a\ne k$, with $t_a\in\{\pm1\}$.
Symmetry implies that $t_a/v_{1a}$ is independent of $a\ne1$.
It follows that $v_{ij}=\alpha s_is_j$ for a nonzero common real $\alpha$
and a sign vector $s$.
For the dense array,
\[
H=3\alpha^2\Bigl(\prod_i s_i\Bigr)D_s(J_6-I)D_s,
\]
whose positive/negative spectral dimensions are $1$ and $5$ in some order.
For the matching array,
\[
H=c^2\Bigl(\prod_{e\in M}\operatorname{sign}v_e\Bigr)M,
\]
which has signature $(3,3)$.  In either case $H$ is invertible, so the two
orthogonal ranges fill $\R^6$ and $A$ must be the corresponding positive or
negative spectral projection.  This gives exactly \eqref{eq:families}.
Conversely, direct substitution verifies equality.  Equality in either scalar
bound with $q_1>0$ forces equality in the operator step; $q_1=0$ is the
diagonal case.
\end{proof}

\section{Collision-free Gaussian boson sampling}\label{sec:gbs}
For a pure, zero-displacement GBS state, collision-free pattern probabilities
have the form~\cite{hamilton,kruse}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gbsprob}
p_B(S)=C(B)|\haf(B[S])|^2,
\end{equation}
where $B$ is complex symmetric with singular values below one and the factor
$C(B)$ is independent of the collision-free pattern $S$.  We restrict here to
real symmetric $B$, the scope of Theorem~\ref{thm:hierarchy}.

\begin{corollary}[Collision-free photon-sector hierarchy]\label{cor:gbs}
Let $B$ be a real symmetric sampling matrix of a pure, zero-displacement
GBS state on $N$ modes, and let $r\ge2$. If
$N\ge4r-2$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gbsavg}
\boxed{
\frac1{\binom N{2r}}\sum_{|S|=2r}p_B(S)
\le\frac{d(B)^2}{4}
\frac1{\binom N{2r-2}}\sum_{|S|=2r-2}p_B(S).
}
\end{equation}
If $P^{\rm cf}_{2r}=\sum_{|S|=2r}p_B(S)$ denotes total collision-free mass in
the $2r$-photon sector, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gbstotal}
P^{\rm cf}_{2r}\le\frac{d(B)^2}{4}
\frac{\binom N{2r}}{\binom N{2r-2}}P^{\rm cf}_{2r-2}.
\end{equation}
In particular, $\|B\|<1$ implies that the average per-pattern probability in
\eqref{eq:gbsavg} contracts by at most $\|B\|^2<1$.  If
$B=O\operatorname{diag}(\tanh s_j)O^T$ with $s_j\ge0$ and a real orthogonal interferometer,
then $B\succeq0$ and the contraction factor is at most $\|B\|^2/4<1/4$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The common factor $C(B)$ cancels from Theorem~\ref{thm:hierarchy}.  For real
symmetric $B$, $d(B)\le2\|B\|$, and for PSD $B$, $d(B)\le\|B\|$.
\end{proof}

The corollary concerns unconditional probabilities and photon-number-resolving,
collision-free patterns.  It does not say that total photon-sector mass must
decrease, because the number of patterns grows with the sector.  It also does
not address collisionful repetitions or threshold-detector Torontonians.
For graph encodings $B=c\Delta$ with real symmetric adjacency $\Delta$, the
average two-photon step is controlled by $c^2d(\Delta)^2/4$, directly linking
the graph spectrum to its GBS subgraph-weight hierarchy.  The fact that GBS
weights collision-free subgraphs by squared hafnians underlies graph
applications such as dense-subgraph search~\cite{arrazola}.

\section{Comparison with adjacent results and scope}\label{sec:comparison}
Roos~\cite{roos}, Theorem~2.3, defines exactly the normalization in
\eqref{eq:G} and proves logarithmic subadditivity.  Equality occurs in his
displayed bound when all off-diagonal entries are identical.  Our recurrence
\eqref{eq:spectralG} instead uses spectral diameter.  The crossover theorem
shows quantitatively that the two bounds dominate in different regimes.
For the critical matching projection, the one-step improvement factor
\eqref{eq:factor} grows like $r$, whereas the balanced rank-one family lies
exactly at equality of the two coefficients.  This is stronger evidence of a
distinct inequality than a comparison at a single small dimension.

Kummer~\cite{kummer}, Lemma~3.5, uses the ordinary unweighted Kneser adjacency
matrix to establish a square-free derivative isomorphism in his work on
spectral linear matrix inequalities.  Feinsilver~\cite{feinsilver} gives
Boolean-lattice $\mathfrak{sl}(2)$ background.  Lindell and Staples~\cite{lindell}
develop multiplicative norm inequalities in zeon algebras; only their
abstract and publisher preview were available for this comparison. Davidson
and Donsig~\cite{davidson} compute Schur multiplier norms of Kneser adjacency
patterns, notably $K(2n+1,n)$ in Theorem~5.5. This is a different norm and
a different operator from $A\mapsto\mathcal T_m(A)$. To our knowledge, the
specific weighted spectral-diameter estimate and the resulting averaged
recurrence have not appeared in the sources examined.

The result does not resolve the project's separate Boolean-cube-normalized
extremal conjecture
\[
\sup_{B\succeq0,\ \max_{x\in\{\pm1\}^6}x^TBx\le1}
|\haf(2\operatorname{offdiag}B)|=\frac1{216}.
\]
A spectral constraint is not a Boolean-cube constraint.  Nor do we claim a
complete equality classification for Theorem~\ref{thm:critical} when $r\ge3$,
or optimal fixed-$N$ constants when $N>4r-2$.

An important correction to an earlier project note is worth recording.  A
conference involution $K$ has $\|\mathcal T_3(K)\|=3/\sqrt5>1$, but its PSD
projection $P=(I+K)/2$ has
$\|\mathcal T_3(P)\|=3/(2\sqrt5)<1$.  Thus it does not refute the PSD operator
bound; the explicit factorizations \eqref{eq:minus}--\eqref{eq:plus} prove that
bound.

The literature review performed for this draft was targeted, not exhaustive.
A specialist priority check remains necessary before replacing phrases such as
``to our knowledge'' with categorical first-discovery language.
